## Long

I'm an MSc Robotics student at the University of Bristol, motivated by a long-standing interest in building intelligent systems that can learn, adapt and operate in the physical world.

My journey into robotics follows more than a decade in software engineering, primarily developing high-performance C++ applications for computer-aided design. Working with computational geometry, spatial data structures, algorithm optimisation and complex software architectures taught me the importance of efficiency, reliability and rigorous problem-solving.

Returning to university has given me the opportunity to connect that engineering background with robotics, artificial intelligence and autonomous decision-making. Through coursework and practical experiments, I am developing my understanding of robot perception, machine vision, motion, sensing and learning-based methods.

My developing research interests focus on multi-agent autonomous systems. I am particularly interested in how robots can learn from experience, evaluate the reliability of information received from other agents and make decisions when their knowledge is incomplete or outdated. Reinforcement learning, continual learning, Age of Information and explainable AI provide several directions I want to investigate.

I am also interested in bringing these ideas into physical robotics, rather than treating autonomy solely as a software problem. I see practical experiments as an important bridge between theoretical models and dependable systems.

My longer-term ambition is to pursue funded PhD research and contribute to the development of adaptive, trustworthy and explainable autonomous robots.
