## 01 · Primary Academic Focus

- **Core areas:** Multi-agent robotics, reinforcement learning (RL), and continual learning.
- **Primary focus:** Multi-agent autonomous systems.
- **Key research question:** How can robots determine whether shared information from other agents is still reliable enough to use?
- **Methods of interest:** Reinforcement learning, Age of Information (AoI), and visual perception.
- **Academic direction:** Develop this area through the MSc dissertation, with the aim of progressing towards PhD research.
- **Current boundary:** Hardware design is interesting, but it is not a current research priority.

## 02 · Active Laboratory Experiments

- **IR sensor spatial footprint:** Investigating the effective sensing area of the reflectance sensor and how different proportions of black/white surface within that footprint affect the measured reflectance value.
- **Robot-based image scanning:** Group project developing a mobile robot that raster-scans a black-and-white image, moves accurately between pixels, detects surface intensity, and reconstructs the scanned image.
- **Future of Robotics editorial:** Exploring how future robots could become more autonomous, adaptive, self-sustaining, explainable, and capable of learning from their environment and other agents.
- **Apple counting using machine vision:** Developing a vision-based method to detect and count apples in images, with attention to object separation, segmentation/detection, and robustness to visual variation.
- **Resistor color-band identification:** Using machine vision to detect resistor color bands and infer the corresponding resistance value from the detected color sequence.
- **Current priority:** All five activities are currently relevant rather than having one dominant experiment.

## 03 · Research & Architectural Bridge

- **Bridge from current experiments to research:** Current work on sensing, robot motion, image scanning, and machine vision builds the practical foundation needed for autonomous multi-agent systems, where each robot must perceive the environment, localise itself, make decisions, and share information with other agents.
- **Most relevant practical foundation:** The robot-based image scanning project is particularly useful because it combines sensing, motion control, positioning, environmental perception, and reconstruction of information from physical measurements.

## 04 · Current Boundaries & Reading

- **Current scope:** Focus on multi-agent autonomy, reinforcement learning, continual learning, visual perception, and information reliability rather than broad robotics hardware development.
- **Near-term boundary:** Build strong fundamentals in perception, robot motion, localization, mapping, and RL before attempting a fully integrated multi-robot learning system.
- **Research boundary:** Keep the MSc dissertation realistically scoped around a small number of interacting components, especially visual perception, AoI-based information reliability, and RL-based exploration/exploitation decisions.
- **Not a current priority:** Mechanical design, advanced electronics, and low-level hardware design are useful background areas but are not the main research direction.
- **Conceptual reading interest:** Broader ideas around explain ability, epistemic reasoning, knowledge reliability, and how autonomous systems can justify what they know and why they act.
- **Current learning strategy:** Use coursework and practical experiments to strengthen fundamentals first, then connect these ideas gradually into the dissertation architecture.
- **Longer-term direction:** Use the MSc as a foundation for PhD-level research in adaptive, decentralized, explainable multi-agent autonomous systems.
